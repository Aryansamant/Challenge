Garibaldi et al. (2013, Science) found wild insects enhance fruit set regardless of honey-bee abundance. Hive dumps do not substitute for wild pollinator habitat. Dainese et al. (2019) showed that biodiversity-mediated pollination and pest control scale with landscape-level semi-natural habitat and on-farm diversification.

IPBES (2016) Pollinators assessment lists land-use homogenization, pesticides, pathogens, and climate as interacting drivers. Neonicotinoid field studies (Woodcock et al. 2017, Science) found reduced wild-bee reproductive success under country-specific, field-realistic exposure.

Design implication: floral strips next to high insecticide regimes can become ecological traps. Sequence pesticide-risk reduction (IPM thresholds, unsprayed bloom windows, review of seed treatments) with habitat. Cereal monocultures create a seasonal floral desert; even a 2–6 m native strip changes the resource phenology of the farm.

Semi-arid wheat systems still benefit: many wild bees are ground-nesters that need untilled, uncompacted micro-sites plus a few native forbs. Grazing and tillage to the field edge remove both.
