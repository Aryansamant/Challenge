Tamburini et al. (2020) meta-analyzed agricultural diversification (rotations, intercropping, cover crops, agroforestry, field margins). Diversification promoted biodiversity and multiple ecosystem services — soil fertility, nutrient cycling, water regulation, pest control — without a systematic yield penalty.

Beillouin et al. (2021, Global Change Biology) confirm the biodiversity effect is robustly positive but heterogeneous: climate, baseline intensification and which practice is used all matter. A generic “plant more species” recommendation underperforms a diagnosis-led mix that attacks the local limiting factors (water, toxicity, isolation, floral dearth).

Kremen and Miles (2012, Ecology and Society) compared biologically diversified and conventional farming: diversified systems supplied more supporting and regulating services, including soil biodiversity and pollination. The implication for declining on-farm biodiversity in a wheat monoculture is direct: the crop itself is a habitat filter. Changing the crop mixture and the off-field habitat is more powerful than adding a single input.
