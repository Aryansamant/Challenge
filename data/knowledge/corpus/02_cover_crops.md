source: Poeplau and Don 2015 Agriculture Ecosystems and Environment

Poeplau and Don (2015) synthesized paired-plot studies of cover crops versus winter fallow. Mean SOC sequestration was 0.32 ± 0.08 Mg C ha−1 yr−1. Accumulation was roughly linear over the observed durations (up to ~50 years), implying no rapid saturation on most agricultural soils.

The mechanism is additional net primary production during the fallow window, especially below-ground. Legume covers add biologically fixed nitrogen, which can increase both cover and subsequent cash-crop residue. Non-legume grasses often produce more biomass in temperate winters. Mixtures frequently outperform sole covers for erosion control and floral resources.

FAO and subsequent reviews note 10–25% relative SOC increases over 2–4 years on carbon-poor soils when covers are combined with residue retention. Biodiversity co-benefits include: (1) living roots feeding rhizosphere microbes through the off-season; (2) flowers supporting pollinators if termination is delayed until bloom; (3) reduced nitrate leaching that otherwise eutrophies downstream habitats.

Constraint: in arid and very dry semi-arid systems, a dense cover can transpire the stored water needed by the staple crop. Sparse, early-terminated legumes, mulch without a full cover, or native weedy fallows may be the water-rational analogue.
