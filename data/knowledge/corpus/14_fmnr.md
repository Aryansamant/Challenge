title: Farmer-managed natural regeneration and dryland restoration

Farmer-managed natural regeneration (FMNR) protects and prunes existing native stumps and rootstock rather than planting nursery trees. Across Niger, Burkina Faso and related Sahelian systems, FMNR has restored parkland tree densities on millions of hectares, with documented gains in fodder, firewood, crop microclimate and bird habitat.

Why it outperforms naive tree-planting on degraded drylands: (1) the genetics are local; (2) roots already reach deep moisture; (3) labor cost is pruning, not watering; (4) farmers choose species they value, so protection persists.

Pairing FMNR with soil-water conservation (zai, stone lines) is the empirically successful bundle. Grazing and fuelwood rules during the first years determine success more than seedling quality. For a biodiversity engine, FMNR is the default woody-cover recommendation wherever remnant rootstock exists and rainfall is arid to sub-humid.
