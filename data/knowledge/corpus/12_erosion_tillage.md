title: Erosion, tillage and the carbon–biodiversity spiral
source: Lal 2003 2004; Powlson et al. 2014 Nature Climate Change
year: 2004
topics: [erosion, soil_organic_carbon, tillage, microbial_diversity]
---

Lal (2003, 2004) quantified how erosion preferentially removes the light, carbon-rich fraction and the seed/microbe bank of the top centimetres. On sloping semi-arid wheat land, erosion can dominate the SOC budget. Stopping soil movement is therefore a biodiversity intervention.

Reduced tillage can help, but Powlson et al. (2014) caution that no-till often redistributes SOC toward the surface rather than adding large new stocks. Biodiversity outcomes depend on whether residue is retained and whether herbicide use rises to compensate. Fungal hyphae and earthworms recover when both physical disturbance and chronic chemical disturbance fall.

A scientifically defensible sequence on eroded, low-SOC cropland:
1. Cover the soil (residue, cover, or even gravel/stone bunds).
2. Slow water (contours).
3. Feed microbes (compost primer + legumes if water allows).
4. Add perennial structure.
Skipping to “no-till plus glyphosate” in a floral desert can raise surface C while insects continue to collapse.
