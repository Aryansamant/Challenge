IPBES (2019) ranks the direct drivers of global biodiversity loss as: (1) land-use change, (2) direct exploitation, (3) climate change, (4) pollution, (5) invasive alien species. Nature’s contributions to people are declining as a result. The assessment rejects single-sector fixes.

Response options with evidence include ecosystem restoration, sustainable agricultural diversification, reducing pollution (nutrients, pesticides, plastics), and maintaining connectivity. The UN Decade on Ecosystem Restoration is aligned with this: restore hydrology and native vegetation, do not merely plant monoculture trees.

Human impact on a given parcel should be scored along at least three axes — conversion (deforestation/drainage), intensification (pesticides, tillage, fertilizers), and isolation (fragmentation). A farm can be “green” in input use yet barren if it sits in a 100% cropped matrix with no habitat. Conversely, high pesticide use can nullify excellent hedgerows.

IPBES IAS (2023) later elevated invasive species control as a professional practice: prioritize pathways and seed sources, revegetate immediately, avoid blanket herbicide that harms natives.
