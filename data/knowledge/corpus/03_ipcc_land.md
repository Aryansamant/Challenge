The IPCC Special Report on Climate Change and Land (SRCCL, 2019) assesses agriculture, forestry and other land use (AFOLU) as both a source of emissions and a reservoir of mitigation/adaptation options. Desertification and land degradation reduce NPP, SOC and biodiversity together; they are not separate problems.

Agroforestry is identified as a response option with medium-to-high confidence for carbon sequestration, livelihood diversification and, when native species are used, habitat provision. Global technical mitigation potentials span a wide range because tree–crop competition, tenure and water availability bound real-world uptake.

Land degradation in drylands is tightly coupled to rainfall variability. Practices that improve infiltration (mulch, bunds, perennial cover) reduce the conversion of drought into desertification. Monocultures of shallow-rooted annuals on low-SOC soils are identified as vulnerable to the very climate shocks that then further reduce biodiversity.

IPCC AR6 WGIII (Chapter 7) reiterates that demand-side and ecosystem-based options should not be framed as yield-versus-nature. Diversified systems can raise resilience of production while supporting species, but only if they do not expand the agricultural frontier.
