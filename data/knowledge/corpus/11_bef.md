Cardinale et al. (2012) summarized two decades of biodiversity–ecosystem function (BEF) research: losing species reduces biomass production, nutrient cycling efficiency and stability. Effects are nonlinear; the first losses in a diverse system are partly buffered, but simplified agroecosystems already sit on the steep part of the curve.

Isbell et al. (2015) showed that more diverse grassland communities resist climate extremes better — droughts and wet anomalies reduce productivity less when many species are present. For land managers facing rising temperature extremes (IPCC AR6 WGII), plant and soil diversity is an adaptation measure, not an aesthetic extra.

Habitat heterogeneity (Tews et al. 2004) is the spatial analogue: structurally mixed vegetation supports more trophic levels. Agroforestry, mixed rotations, and uncropped patches are how farms buy back heterogeneity without abandoning production.

Recommendation quality test: if a suggested practice only names “sustainability” without a BEF or water mechanism, it is not evidence-backed.
