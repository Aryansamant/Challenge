title: Soil pH as a master variable of microbial diversity
source: Fierer and Jackson 2006 PNAS
year: 2006
topics: [soil_ph, microbial_diversity, nutrient_cycling]
---

Fierer and Jackson (2006) showed that soil pH is the strongest continental-scale predictor of soil bacterial diversity and richness. Neutral soils host more diverse bacterial communities than strongly acidic ones. This is not a minor correlation: pH filters whole clades via aluminum toxicity, membrane physiology and nutrient speciation.

Below pH ~5.5, phosphorus is fixed onto Fe/Al oxides, aluminum becomes phytotoxic, and many crops plus their rhizobia fail. Above ~8.3, iron, zinc and phosphorus availability collapse and sodicity may disperse aggregates.

For biodiversity recommendations, pH is a gate. Cover crops and compost underperform if the microbial community cannot persist. Lime (acid soils) or gypsum plus organic matter (sodic alkaline soils) can be the highest-leverage first action. Overliming is harmful; rates must follow a buffer test.

Wagg et al. (2014, PNAS) linked soil biodiversity to ecosystem multifunctionality — nutrient cycling, decomposition, plant productivity — so restoring the abiotic envelope (pH, moisture, carbon) is restoring the stage on which species act.
