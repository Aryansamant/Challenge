Noy-Meir’s pulse-reserve paradigm (1973) still structures dryland ecology: rainfall arrives in pulses; plants and microbes persist on reserves. Maestre et al. (2012, Science) showed that dryland ecosystem multifunctionality increases with species richness — diversity is not a luxury in deserts; it stabilizes function under stress.

Isbell et al. (2015, Nature) found biodiversity increases resistance of ecosystem productivity to climate extremes in grassland experiments. The reverse is also true: simplified wheat monocultures on 0.3% SOC soils have almost no reserve of water or biological insurance.

Operational levers in semi-arid lands:
1. Raise infiltration (contour bunds, zai pits, residue mulch ≥30% cover).
2. Raise SOC slightly to lift available water capacity (Hudson 1994).
3. Reintroduce perennial structure (FMNR, Faidherbia) to shade soil and tap deep water.
4. Avoid dense off-season covers that out-compete the staple for stored water.

Sawadogo and WOCAT/FAO documentation of zai + compost + stone bunds in Burkina Faso show co-recovery of woody cover, crop yield and soil life on crusted plateaus. The causal chain is water → plants → litter → SOC → more water, not a single-variable soil test correction.
